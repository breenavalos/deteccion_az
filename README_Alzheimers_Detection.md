## 🌿 The Story Behind the Project

This project began with a deeply personal motivation: my grandmother's experience with Alzheimer's disease.

Witnessing the impact of the disease on individuals and their families inspired me to explore how technology could contribute to healthcare in a meaningful way. For my undergraduate thesis at **Universidad de Morón**, I decided to investigate the potential of **artificial intelligence in the early detection of Alzheimer's disease**.

The project brought together two areas that continue to drive my professional interests: **AI and healthcare**. It also marked an important milestone in my academic journey, leading to a research contribution presented at **CACIC 2024**, the Argentine Congress of Computer Science.

> Technology becomes especially meaningful when it is developed with people in mind.

## 🔬 Project Overview

**Early Alzheimer's Detection Using AI Classification Models** explores the use of artificial intelligence and image-classification approaches to support the earlier identification of patterns associated with Alzheimer's disease.

The broader thesis also included a **systematic mapping study of the scientific literature**, examining existing AI approaches, reported diagnostic performance, challenges in medical research, and privacy considerations related to healthcare data.

### Research objectives

- Explore the potential of machine learning and medical image classification in Alzheimer's research.
- Review scientific evidence on AI-assisted early detection.
- Examine relevant evaluation methods and challenges in applying AI to healthcare.
- Investigate how technological solutions may support, rather than replace, clinical decision-making.

## ⚕️ Responsible AI in Healthcare

AI-based classification systems may support medical research, but performance in an experimental setting does not automatically translate into clinical reliability.

Important considerations include **privacy**, **representativeness of training data**, **potential bias**, **external validation**, **explainability**, and **appropriate clinical oversight**.

**Disclaimer:** This is an academic research project, not a certified medical device. It is not intended to diagnose Alzheimer's disease or replace qualified healthcare professionals.
